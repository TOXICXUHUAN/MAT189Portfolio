● Done. Added a section rail, a back-to-top button, and the script that drives
  them.

  What's there

  - script.js (new) — builds the nav from whatever <section class="project">
  blocks exist in <main>, so adding a project to the HTML adds it to the nav
  automatically. It marks the section you're on as you scroll (a section takes
  over once its top crosses the upper third of the screen; the bottom of the
  page hands the highlight to the last one), and shows the top button after the
  first screen.
  - index.html — a <nav class="rail"> with an empty list for the script to fill,
  an id on each section for stable deep links, and the back-to-top anchor.
  - style.css:383-670 (style.css#L383-L670) — new sections 8 and 9, using the
  existing tokens (mono uppercase labels, the ember accent,
  --surface/--line/--shadow, rem/clamp sizing), so both themes come along for
  free.

  Design choices worth knowing

  The rail isn't painted over your prose — at ≥75em it gets its own column via
  --rail on body's padding, and --shell re-centers in what's left. Below that,
  --rail is 0 and the same list folds into a panel behind an "Index" pill at the
  bottom-left, so nothing shifts on a phone. The marker is the little rule
  beside each entry: it grows and picks up the accent on the current section,
  and the 01, 02, 03… numbers echo the running counter on your headings.

  Verified in Edge across 8 scroll positions (correct section marked at each,
  nothing marked while you're still on the header), at 390 / 700 / 1050 / 1200 /
  1210 / 1440 / 1500px, in both palettes, and over file:// as well as a server.
  Clicking an entry jumps and closes the panel; the top button returns to the
  header. Escape, outside taps, and resizing past the breakpoint all close the
  panel too.

  One bug the sweep caught and I fixed: at exactly 1200px both breakpoint blocks
  matched and the panel's visibility: hidden leaked onto the standing rail,
  blanking it. The wide block now explicitly undoes the panel, so the tie always
  resolves to the rail — the two queries meet at 75em with no gap either.

  Two judgment calls, easy to change if you'd rather not: the rail has no "Top"
  entry, since the button covers it, so nothing is highlighted while you're
  still on the header; and long titles wrap rather than truncate — "Randomly
  Generated Artwork Program" runs to three or four lines at narrower desktop
  widths.
