  Space usage. The max-width: 42rem centered column is gone. Content now sits in
  a --shell of min(100% - gutters, 90rem), and each project becomes a 12-column
  grid above 60em: title left / meta right on the top row, then figures, video,
  and prose flowing two-up beneath it. On your 1440p screen that means images
  sit side by side at roughly 880px each instead of stacking down a narrow
  strip. The Lantern section works out especially well — three images plus the
  description fill a clean 2×2 block.

  No absolute units. Everything is rem, em, ch, %, vw/vh, or clamp(). The root
  font size is itself fluid (clamp(1rem, 0.95rem + 0.22vw, 1.25rem)), so it
  lands near 16px on a phone and 20px at 2560px wide, and every rem-based size
  scales with it. Hairline borders are 0.0625rem rather than 1px.

  New look.
  - Warm ember accent (#e0a04a) on cold charcoal, with a fixed radial wash
  behind the page so the wide layout isn't a flat slab. A prefers-color-scheme: 
  light block flips the whole palette to warm paper — swap the two token blocks
  if you want light as the default.
  - Three type stacks instead of one: a serif display for headings, a neutral
  sans for body, and mono for meta lines, captions, and the footer (uppercase,
  letterspaced). All system fonts, so no network request.
  - Captions get an accent rule on their left edge; images get rounded corners,
  a shadow, and a small lift on hover; the "Play The Descent" link renders as a
  pill button with an arrow.
  - Focus-visible outlines, selection colors, and a prefers-reduced-motion
  block.

  Two things worth knowing:

  1. I added a decorative CSS counter that prints 01, 02, … above each project
  title. It's generated content, not markup — the commented block at
  style.css:180 (MAT189/portfolio/style.css#L180) is the only thing to delete if
  you don't want it.
  2. Unrelated to styling, but I noticed while listing the media folder: the
  HTML asks for chen_beachdemo_02.jpg and chen_turtle_03.png, while the files on
  disk are .JPG and .PNG. Windows doesn't care, but GitHub Pages is
  case-sensitive and those two images will 404 once deployed. I left it alone
  since you asked me not to touch the elements — say the word and I'll fix the
  extensions.

● Added, replacing the old neutral purple/blue wash.

  How it's built. body::before is a fixed full-viewport layer with three stacked
  gradients:

  - a 135deg linear gradient carrying deep red out of the top-left to orange in
  the bottom-right, going fully transparent between 42% and 58%;
  - a radial bloom anchored at 0% 0% in the same red;
  - a radial bloom anchored at 100% 100% in the orange.

  The two radials sit on top of the linear's endpoints, so the corners read as
  light radiating from them while the diagonal ties them together. Everything
  fades out before the middle third, which is exactly where your project text
  lives.

  Keeping it readable. The whole layer is capped by opacity: 
  var(--tint-strength) rather than baking alpha into each color — one number
  governs the entire effect. At the current 0.18, the hottest point on the page
  (the top-left corner, under your name) composites to about #2e1313, which puts
  --ink at roughly 13:1 contrast and the muted mono labels around 6:1. Both
  clear WCAG AA comfortably. Light mode gets its own softer pair at 0.15,
  landing on a pale peach.

  Tuning. Three tokens at style.css:31-33 (MAT189/portfolio/style.css#L31-L33):
  --tint-a (top-left), --tint-b (bottom-right), --tint-strength. Bump strength
  to ~0.26 if you want it more assertive — I'd stay under about 0.3 before the
  muted caption text starts to suffer. Flip the two colors to reverse the
  direction, or change 135deg to 45deg to run bottom-left → top-right instead.
  Light mode has its own set at style.css:69-71
  (MAT189/portfolio/style.css#L69-L71).

  Since it's position: fixed, the wash stays put as you scroll rather than
  sliding past — the corners stay lit the whole way down. If you'd rather it
  travel with the page so the orange only arrives at the bottom of the document,
  that's a one-word change to position: absolute, though it needs the corner
  blooms re-tuned since the layer becomes very tall.
